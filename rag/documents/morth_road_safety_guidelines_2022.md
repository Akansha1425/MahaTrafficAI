# MoRTH Guidelines for Road Safety & Identification of Black Spots (2022)

**Authorizing Body:** Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH), Government of India  
**Document Code:** MoRTH/RS-2022/BS-01  
**Target Domain:** National Highways and State Arterials  
**Relevant State:** Maharashtra (Applies nationally, referenced by MaHa PWD)

---

## 1. Definition and Protocol for Road Accident Black Spots

Under MoRTH and Indian Roads Congress (IRC) standards, a **Road Accident Black Spot** is defined as a road stretch of about **500 meters** in length in which either:
1. **5 or more fatal accidents** have occurred in the preceding three calendar years, OR
2. **10 or more fatalities** have occurred in the preceding three calendar years.

### Remedial Engineering Measures
- Immediate short-term interventions: High-visibility thermoplastic road markings, rumble strips before hazardous junctions, retro-reflective cautionary signage, and electronic speed display boards.
- Medium to long-term interventions: Junction geometric redesign, construction of vehicular underpasses (VUP) or pedestrian underpasses (PUP), installation of W-beam crash barriers, and grade-separated intersections.
- Maharashtra has identified over 450 black spots on state and national highways within the state as of 2022.

---

## 2. Speed Management Guidelines on National & State Highways

- **Design Speed Limits:**
  - Access-controlled expressways: 120 km/h for light motor vehicles (LMVs), 80 km/h for heavy commercial vehicles (HCVs).
  - 4-lane divided National Highways: 100 km/h for LMVs, 80 km/h for HCVs.
  - Undivided 2-lane State Highways: 70 km/h for LMVs, 60 km/h for HCVs.
  - Urban arterial roads and school zones: 40 km/h and 25 km/h respectively.
- **Enforcement Mechanisms:** Sectional speed detection cameras over 5 km intervals rather than single-point radar guns to discourage sudden braking.
- Maharashtra Traffic Police uses Interceptor vehicle-mounted LIDAR guns for mobile speed enforcement on expressways.

---

## 3. Monsoon & Adverse Weather Safety Mandates

- **Drainage and Skid Resistance:** All highway concessionaires must inspect longitudinal drains before June 1 annually. Minimum skid resistance value of 55 BPN (British Pendulum Number) required on wet pavement surfaces.
- **Pothole Repair Timelines:** Potholes emerging on National Highway networks must be filled with cold-mix or mastic asphalt within 48 hours of detection during rainy periods.
- During June-September monsoon season, Maharashtra typically sees a 30-35% spike in road accident fatalities compared to dry months due to skidding on wet roads and reduced visibility.

---

## 4. Maharashtra-Specific Accident Corridors

- **Mumbai-Pune Expressway (NH-48):** Among the highest accident density expressways. Common causes: overspeeding, fog patches in Khandala Ghat section, tire burst.
- **Nashik-Pune Highway (NH-60):** Significant truck-LMV collision corridor.
- **Nagpur-Amravati-Yavatmal Corridor:** High fatality rate among Vidarbha region roads due to unlit stretches.
- **Satara-Kolhapur (NH-48 South):** Frequent accidents at Karad bypass junctions.

---

## 5. Fatality Classification per MoRTH

| Classification | Definition |
|---|---|
| Fatal Accident | Any accident resulting in death within 30 days |
| Serious Injury | Hospitalization for > 24 hours |
| Minor Injury | Hospitalization for < 24 hours or outpatient treatment |
| Non-injury | Property damage only |

---

## 6. Risk Score Computation Methodology (MoRTH Research Wing)

MoRTH-affiliated research institutions use composite risk scoring:
- **Accident Severity Index (ASI):** (3 × Fatal + 1 × Serious + 0.3 × Minor) / Total Accidents
- **Accident Rate per MVKM:** (Number of accidents / Vehicle Kilometers Traveled) × 10^6
- Locations scoring > 3 on ASI and > 0.5 accidents/MVKM are classified as high-priority intervention zones.
