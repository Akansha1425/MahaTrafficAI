# Maharashtra Motor Vehicles Rules — Road Safety Summary

**Source:** Maharashtra Motor Vehicles Rules 1989 (Amended through 2023)  
**Administering Authority:** Maharashtra Transport Department (RTO)  
**Enforcement Agency:** Maharashtra Traffic Police

---

## 1. Licensing & Driver Competency Requirements

- **Commercial Vehicle Drivers (HMV/Transport):** Minimum 5 years experience in LMV before HMV license. Medical fitness certificate mandatory annually after age 50.
- **Learner's License:** L-board display mandatory. Cannot drive on expressways. Speed limit capped at 40 km/h.
- **High-Security Registration Plates (HSRP):** Mandatory for all vehicles registered in Maharashtra from 2019 onwards.

## 2. Traffic Violation Fine Structure (Maharashtra, 2023)

| Violation | Fine (INR) |
|---|---|
| Drunken driving (first offense) | ₹10,000 |
| Drunken driving (repeat) | ₹15,000 + 2 years imprisonment |
| Overspeeding (LMV) | ₹1,000–₹2,000 |
| Overspeeding (HMV) | ₹2,000–₹4,000 |
| Red light jumping | ₹1,000–₹5,000 |
| Helmet non-compliance (2-wheeler) | ₹1,000 |
| Seatbelt non-compliance | ₹1,000 |
| Using mobile while driving | ₹5,000 |
| Dangerous driving | ₹5,000 |
| No third-party insurance | ₹2,000 |

## 3. Maharashtra Road Safety Policy Priorities (2022-2027)

1. **Vision Zero** pilot program in Pune Metropolitan Region to eliminate fatal accidents by 2027.
2. **Helmet enforcement** — 100% helmet compliance target for all 2-wheeler riders and pillion passengers.
3. **Speed cameras** — Installation of 2,400 speed detection cameras across NH and SH by 2025.
4. **Drunk driving enforcement** — Night checking nakas operational every Friday/Saturday at 22 identified high-risk zones.
5. **Black spot remediation** — ₹850 crore budget allocated for engineering improvements at 450 black spots (2022-2025).

---

## 4. Statistical Context (Maharashtra, 2022-2023)

- Maharashtra accounts for approximately **9-10%** of all road accident fatalities in India, making it one of the top 5 states by fatality count.
- Two-wheelers constitute approximately **40%** of road accident fatalities in Maharashtra.
- Pedestrian fatalities account for **18-22%** of total deaths.
- Peak accident hours: **6 PM – 10 PM** (evening rush + reduced visibility).
- Peak accident months: **June–September** (monsoon) and **December** (festival/fog season).

---

## 5. Road Safety Audit Requirements

- All new highway projects must undergo a road safety audit at 4 stages: Feasibility, Detailed Design, Pre-opening, and Post-opening (within 12 months).
- Auditors must be IRC-certified Road Safety Auditors with minimum 3 years experience.
- Audit reports must be submitted to NHAI/MaHa PWD within 60 days of field inspection.

---

## 6. Accident Reporting & Investigation Protocol

1. **FIR Filing:** Police file First Information Report (FIR) within 24 hours of fatal accident.
2. **Spot Inspection:** Traffic police and PWD engineers must jointly inspect fatal accident sites within 7 days.
3. **MVCR Reporting:** Motor Vehicle Crash Report (MVCR) filed with MoRTH within 30 days.
4. **IRAD Integration:** Maharashtra integrated with India Road Accident Database (iRAD) portal from 2021. All fatal accidents captured on iRAD by traffic police at scene.
